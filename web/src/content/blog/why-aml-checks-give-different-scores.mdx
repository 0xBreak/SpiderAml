Maria checked the same wallet in two services. The first answered “0, low risk”. The second “30%, medium risk”. She was about to accept money from that wallet today. Which answer should she trust? (The example is made up, but anyone who has checked an address in more than one place will recognise it.)

Short version: **a risk score is not a measurement like temperature, it is a model's conclusion from data.** Every service has its own data, its own rules and its own scale, so the same address can honestly be rated differently. Neither answer has to be wrong. Below: what any score is made of, five reasons results differ, how to read a report without being fooled by a neat number, and what to do when answers disagree.

## What any risk score is made of

Whatever is printed in a report, the number comes from three steps, and two services can part ways at any of them:

- **Data.** What the service knows about other people's addresses: which are marked as sanctioned, stolen, fraudulent, whose they are (exchange, service, private person).
- **Rules.** What counts as risky and how much: a direct contact, a link through intermediaries, the size of the amounts, how recent it is.
- **Scale.** The units of the answer: points from 0 to 100, percentages, levels such as “low / medium / high”.

Next, where exactly this leads to different answers.

## Five reasons results differ

**1. The services have different data.** Labels are gathered from different places: official sanctions lists, victims' reports, analysts' observations, a service's own investigations. An address labelled at one service may simply be unknown to another. Note: “no labels” means “we know nothing about this address”, not “the address is clean”.

**2. A direct link or one several steps away.** Suppose the wallet being checked has no labels near it, but three steps back the money comes from a labelled address. One approach looks only at the nearest labels and sees zero. Another counts what share of the money travelled along the chain and gets a noticeable figure. A third treats a distant link as a weak hint. All three are legitimate, and in the picture below they give three different answers for the same path of money.

![One path of money, three ways to rate it](/blog/aml-hops.en.svg?v=1)

**3. “Thirty” can mean different things.** “30 out of 100” is a model's score. “30%” can be the share of amounts that travelled along a chain from a labelled address, and then it is not at all a “probability of fraud”. The same numbers, different meanings. So you cannot compare figures from two reports without checking what is being measured underneath.

**4. Different time and different network.** Data changes daily: labels are added and addresses are frozen. We read such events straight from the blockchain, and a check made yesterday may show something else today. A service may also have looked at only one network: a person's addresses on Ethereum, Tron and Bitcoin are different addresses with different histories.

**5. Different questions and different policy.** One tool checks a wallet, another a specific transaction, a third sanctions lists. And the decision to accept a deposit is made not by a tool but by the platform under its own rules: a “low risk” from a checker does not oblige an exchange to accept the money.

## How to read any AML report

Instead of looking only at the number, go through these questions:

1. **What was checked:** a wallet, a transaction or a list? Which network?
2. **When:** is the date and time of the check given?
3. **Where the number comes from:** are reasons named (risk category, addresses, amounts), or is there one score?
4. **Direct or indirect link:** contact with whom, through how many steps?
5. **What the number measures:** a score, a share of amounts or a level?
6. **Is there a sanctions, theft or hack signal:** that is a separate and the most serious signal, more important than an average score.
7. **What the report cannot see:** does it note the depth of the check and the networks covered?

## Early warning signs

A report or a service should not be trusted blindly if:

- only a number is shown, with no reasons or sources;
- there is no date and no network;
- it promises “100% clean” or “we guarantee the exchange will not block it”;
- it does not tell a direct link from an indirect one;
- “medium risk” is written without a category;
- you are offered a paid “cleaning” or “whitelisting” of funds;
- you are asked for a seed phrase or private key “to run the check”.

## What to do when answers disagree

1. **Do not average.** Zero and thirty do not make fifteen: they answer different questions.
2. **Make sure the same thing was checked:** one address, one network, close in time.
3. **Compare reasons, not numbers:** categories, direct or indirect link, amounts.
4. **If any report shows sanctions, theft or a hack,** deal with that before you accept or send money.
5. **For a large amount rely on the more cautious answer** and ask the counterparty for the transaction hash and proof of the source of funds.
6. **Remember the platform:** an exchange has its own rules, and its decision does not have to match an outside check.

## What to do if you already accepted or sent money based on a check

1. Save the reports with the date, address and network, and the transaction hashes.
2. If an exchange asks for documents, prepare proof of the source of funds: a contract, correspondence, a receipt.
3. Do not pay “cleaning specialists” and never send anyone keys or a seed phrase.
4. In a dispute over a large amount, see a lawyer: the procedure depends on the jurisdiction.

## FAQ

**Why does the same wallet get different risk in different services?** The services have different data, rules and scales, and they check at different times. Answers can differ without anyone being wrong.

**What does “30% risk” mean?** It depends on the service. It can be a model's score, or the share of amounts that travelled along a chain from a labelled address. Read the description under the number.

**If a check shows 0, is the address safe?** No. It means the service's sources hold no signals. The address may be new, unknown or only recently labelled.

**Which service is more accurate?** You cannot tell from the number. Look at whether reasons are named, whether date and network are given, and whether sources are shown.

**Can two results be averaged?** No, they are different measurements. Better to compare the reasons and take the more cautious conclusion.
